Upload a k-plexity curve, the fraction of unique k-mers in an assembly as a function of k, to fit the kplexity model and compare your genome with Darwin Tree of Life assemblies. The model separates chance collisions of short k-mers, near copies (repeats whose copies differ, which become unique as k grows) and identical copies (never resolved). Reading the parameters

What each kplexity-model parameter does and does not tell you about a genome. Back to the explorer

y(k) = B(k) · [A − S · e−λ(k − k0)],   B(k) = (1 − e−L) / L,   L = 2 e−b(k − k0)

B(k) is the chance-collision term: short k-mers recur by chance, and with b = ln 4 and k0 = log4(genome size) B is the exact curve of random sequence. The bracket is what is left once chance collisions are gone: the plateau A, minus the near copies still shared, a share S at k0 that decays as k grows because a longer k-mer is more likely to span a difference between copies. In the explorer, the dashed curve is B·A and the shaded band between it and the fit is the near-copy term.

Each reading below comes with the evidence behind it: the 323 reference genomes (repeat content from EDTA for transposable elements and TRASH for satellites) and simulated 500-kb genomes where the repeat content is known. Open a panel to see the plot. Once you load a curve, your value is drawn as a black line.

k0 · first-rise position
The k at which chance collisions stop and most of the genome becomes unique. In random sequence this is log4(genome size). Across the reference genomes k0 tracks log4(genome size) closely (r = ; median k0 − log4G = ). It is an internal control: a k0 far from log4(genome size) points to a problem with the curve or with the genome size.

S · near-copy share at k0
The share of k-mers that are still shared between repeat copies at k0 but become unique at larger k, because the copies differ. Across the reference genomes S tracks the share of the genome annotated as transposable elements (r = ; S ≈ × TE share, fitted through the origin). Genomes rich in satellites and genomes with many duplicated genes sit above the line (r = between satellite content and the distance above the line), since their near copies are not transposable elements. S is not total repeat content: copies that differ a lot are already unique at k0 and add little to S. In simulation, with 30% of the genome in repeat copies, S is divergence between copies.

With 30% in 5-kb copies (open squares, divergence 0–40%), S is close to the share when copies have barely diverged and near zero when they differ by 30% or more. Dashed line: equality.

A and 1 − A · identical copies
A is the plateau: the fraction of k-mers that some k resolves. The rest, 1 − A, is in copies so similar that no k up to the last one separates them. In simulation, 1 − A equals the share of the genome in perfect tandem arrays (, largest difference ). In the reference genomes (median 1 − A = ) it correlates with satellite content (r = ) and more weakly with transposable-element content (r = ). It reflects the most homogeneous copies (e.g. rDNA, homogenised satellite arrays) rather than the total amount of repeats, and it can only count copies present in the assembly. When the curve has not levelled off by its last k, A is an extrapolation and the explorer says so.

divergence · between near copies
How different the near copies are: divergence = 1 − e−λ, where λ is the rate at which the near-copy share decays with k. A k-mer in a copy becomes unique once it spans a difference, with probability 1 − (1 − divergence)k. In simulation the fitted divergence follows the true one when copies are similar ( true divergence) and runs above it for more diverged copies (). In the reference genomes it is mostly 1–5% (median %, 5th–95th percentile –%). A real genome mixes repeat families of different ages, so read it as the divergence of the near copies that dominate S, not of all repeats.

The double sigmoid (earlier versions)

Earlier versions of the tool fitted y = L1/(1 + e−s1(k − k0_1)) + L2/(1 + e−s2(k − k0_2)). It is still available in the explorer (Model: double sigmoid), and the reference genomes carry both fits.

  • k0_1: first transition, about log4(genome size); corresponds to k0.
  • s1: steepness of the first transition; corresponds to b.
  • L2: share of k-mers that become unique only at a second, later transition; tracks transposable-element content, like S.
  • k0_2, s2: position and steepness of the second transition; in simulation k0_2 falls as copies diverge. They describe the shape of the curve rather than a measured quantity.
  • L1: share that becomes unique at the first transition.
  • asymptote = L1 + L2: fraction unique at the longest k; 1 − asymptote corresponds to 1 − A.

A curve was called double when the uniqueness gained after k = 25 was at least 0.05 and k0_2/k0_1 ≥ 2, single when neither held; with no distinct second transition L2, s2 and k0_2 have no meaning. The kplexity model has no such case: S simply goes to zero.
